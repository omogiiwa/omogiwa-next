01
The brief

One brand, four businesses.

What if one brand could be a fashion store, a design store, an app and a film studio, and still feel like one thing?

The name does the first piece of work: Ododo means flower. Everything that follows had to grow from that, without ever tying the brand to a single industry.

02
The first idea

Five rings, two stems.

The idea started as a sketch: five rings in a row, two of them with a stem. Read aloud it is the name: o · d · o · d · o. Simple enough to draw anywhere, and with a rhythm that repeats.

Why a ring?

LensAlways looking, from every side.
ButtonA fashion detail, a tap in an app.
CoinA shop, a currency, a price.
FrameA design, a window, a screen.
ReelFilm, motion, a loop.
03
Exploration

Six rounds of the wordmark.

I kept the rings and tested how to arrange them: spacing, weight, rhythm and colour. Each round was judged on one question: does it still read as a name, and does it still work small?

Round 01
Shortlisted

Open rings

Light, even strokes. Elegant but quiet; the word had no centre of gravity.

Round 02
Chosen

Heavy chain

One heavy stroke, rings touching. It read as a single solid block.

Round 03
Dropped

Pulse

Solid discs alternating with rings. A clever rhythm, but it broke the chain and felt heavy.

Round 04
Dropped

Interlock

Rings overlapping with an over-under weave and an orange lens. Too busy; the letters stopped reading.

Round 05
Dropped

Rising rings

Drawn from my own sketch: each ring a little bigger and higher, in purple. Charming, but the letters lost uniformity.

Round 06
Shortlisted

Close rings

Identical rings on one baseline, lightly touching. Clean and even, but it lacked a point of view.

Shortlist

Open rings, Heavy chain, Close rings

Three directions survived: light and open (A), heavy and tangent (B) and even and close (C).

Decision

Heavy chain

Option B had presence. One stroke weight and tangent rings made the word read as a single solid block.

Next question

How does a name begin?

With a capital. That changed the first ring, and with it the whole logo.

04
The capital O

A name starts with a capital.

I enlarged the first ring so its outer height equals the full ascender height: its top lines up with the stem tops, and its bottom sits on the same baseline as every other ring. Same stroke, bigger ring. Suddenly the word had an entry point, and a standalone monogram for avatars and stamps.

Four capital-O versions were tested against the lowercase original: a dot in the centre ring, the dot moved into the capital, a solid orange capital, and a heavier stroke. The capital that holds the lens won.

05
The flower

Ododo means flower.

The meaning had to appear without breaking the logo I had already built. The rule: the silhouette stays. Rings, stems and capital O are untouched; the flower lives only in the orange accent or one small detail, and falls back to a plain dot when the logo is small.

Motion

The bloom opens first.

The logo animates in the order it was designed: the bloom appears small, the capital O draws round it as the bloom opens, then d, o, d, o follow. As a loader, the eight lobes light up one after another.

240 msbloom opens
120 msbetween letters
1.3 stotal, hold included
100 msper lobe in the loader

Easing is cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1) for every move: no bounce, no spin. With reduced motion switched on, the finished logo appears with no animation.

15
Reflection

What I took from it.

01

Keep the silhouette, move the meaning

The flower arrived last, as a hint. Because the rings were settled first, adding meaning never meant redrawing the logo.

02

One accent is a discipline

A single orange point forced a decision in every layout: where does the eye land first?

03

Test small first

Every variation was checked at 16 to 90 pixels before it earned a page. What survived small survived everywhere.

04

Say no out loud

I set aside four of six wordmark rounds and 22 palettes. The strongest decision was returning to black and orange.
